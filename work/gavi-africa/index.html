GAVI Africa: a website that books car services, not just shows them

GAVI AFRICA LIMITED runs an auto centre in Abule-Egba, Lagos, covering vehicle sales, after-sales service and genuine spare parts. I designed the website, built it and launched it, then sorted out the domain so the company actually owns it.

The job

Three businesses under one roof

GAVI sells cars, services cars and supplies parts. Each has a different customer and a different next step, and most people visiting have never been to the centre. The site had to do three things:

  • Get people to book a service, the main action on the site.
  • Show vehicles for sale in a way the GAVI team can keep up to date without me.
  • Earn trust from someone deciding whether to hand over their car.

I worked from wireframes, taking the homepage through four versions before building.

Decision 01

Lead with service, give each pillar its own path

The hero leads with service and has one main button, Book a Service, with “browse vehicles for sale” as a quieter secondary link. Below it, the three pillars each get a card with what’s included and their own next step: explore vehicles, view services or request parts. A buyer and a fleet manager both find their route within one scroll.

Decision 02

A booking form that does the advisor’s first call

The form asks for what a service advisor needs before the car arrives, so the follow-up call is shorter and the right parts can be ready.

  • Vehicle brand, model and year are required. The VIN is optional, with a note saying why it helps.
  • Drop-off options match how the centre works: standard, Early Bird from 6:30 AM, Saturday morning, or mobile workshop and recovery.
  • Other ways in. The Service Advisor’s number sits next to the form, and a WhatsApp button is always on screen, because many customers would rather message or call.
  • Expectations first. A four-step strip (Book, Diagnose, Service, Drive away) says what happens after submitting.

Submissions go to the team through a Google Apps Script backend, with no paid form service to maintain.

Decision 03

Trust for a place people haven’t visited

Handing your car to a workshop you’ve never seen is a leap. The page answers that with evidence rather than claims: a marquee of the OEM brands GAVI works with, real customer reviews linking to Google, photos of the actual facility and team, the address, opening hours and a map, and an FAQ that answers questions like “I bought my car elsewhere. Can you still service it?”

Decision 04

Content the team can update themselves

Vehicle stock changes often, so it couldn’t live in code. I set up Sanity as the content system, with models for vehicles, blog posts, categories and team members. The GAVI team adds a car or a post in Sanity, and the Astro site renders it. Pages are static and fast, hosted on Vercel.

Beyond the design

Getting the domain back

At launch we found the domain had been registered through a reseller, under the reseller’s details instead of GAVI’s. Pointing it at the new site broke the old email setup, and the reseller asked for a fee to release the transfer code.

I worked through it with the client: we got the transfer code, moved the domain to a registrar under GAVI’s control, and moved the company’s 15 mailboxes to Zoho Mail. The site now runs on a domain GAVI owns.

Where it stands

Live at gaviafrica.com

The site is live with a working booking form, and the GAVI team can add vehicles and posts themselves.
